# Folded Signal

Folded Signal treats transformation as a quiet physical event: a dense field of source marks passes through one decisive fold and emerges as a composed page. Space is not filler but the medium that makes the change legible. Forms are frontal, geometric, and few; each edge is positioned with meticulous craft so the object feels inevitable rather than decorated.

The palette is built from warm paper, near-black ink, and one vermilion registration mark. Color behaves like a printing process rather than a screen effect. The accent appears only where state changes—from raw notation to reading surface—while subtle grain gives the field material presence. Every tone must be painstakingly calibrated to remain distinct on both light and dark surrounding interfaces.

Scale creates the rhythm. A single folded page carries the visual identity; small monospaced marks act like proofing annotations around it. Repetition is sparse and systematic, suggesting lines of Markdown without illustrating syntax literally. The mark should survive reduction to sixteen pixels because its silhouette, not its detail, carries the idea.

Composition balances strict alignment with one controlled displacement: the orange fold slips beyond the black sheet by a few units, signaling motion and automation. Nothing else competes. Typography is rare, clinical, and subordinate to form—only the name or a short functional phrase may appear in larger canvases.

The finished work must feel like the result of countless refinements by a master print designer. Corners, optical weight, transparent padding, and negative space demand painstaking attention. The restraint is the evidence of craft: no gratuitous symbols, no glossy gradients, and no visual element that does not explain transformation.
